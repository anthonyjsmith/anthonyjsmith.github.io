'Intinction' means 'dipping', and it refers to the practice of dipping the bread (usually a wafer) into the wine as part of the celebration of the Lord's Supper (Holy Communion, the Eucharist, the Mass).

I've been dipping into the topic a little, far enough to gather a short reading list. I'm unlikely to take it much further any time soon, so I thought I'd deposit my notes here for future reference. It might even be useful to someone else, who knows.

First, a brief summary...

There are very few references to intinction in the early centuries of church history. It [might have been introduced](https://www.midamerica.edu/uploads/files//pdf/journal/06keisterjournal2018.pdf) in some places as a way of making the bread easier to swallow for the sick or for young children. It is difficult to know when the practice arose, but in the Eastern Orthodox Church today, the bread and wine are mixed together and then served using a spoon. Intinction was a controversial practice in the Western (Roman) Church, and it was banned in the thirteenth century. It seems to have become more popular since the early twentieth century, [in response to growing awareness of the role of germs in spreading diseases](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-9809.70010).

In **dictionaries and encyclopedias**, there are brief entries on 'Intinction' in _A New Dictionary of Liturgy and Worship_ (SCM) (and presumably also in its successor, _The New SCM Dictionary of Liturgy and Worship_), in _The Oxford Dictionary of the Christian Church_, and in [_An Episcopal Dictionary of the Church_](https://www.episcopalchurch.org/glossary/intinction/). [Wikipedia also has an article on the topic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intinction).

In the **Church of England**, current practice is that intinction is generally discouraged but not actually prohibited in the Church of England as a whole, although it is prohibited in quite a few dioceses. Examples of diocesan guidance in which intinction is strongly discouraged include:

* Diocese in Europe: ['A note about receiving Holy Communion by intinction'](https://www.europe.anglican.org/note-about-receiving-holy-communion-intinction)
* Diocese of London: 'Intinction of wafers' as part of some [Communion guidelines](https://www.london.anglican.org/clergy-llm-support/clergy-guides/life-events/communion-guidelines/)

The Church of England's Legal Advisory Commission of the General Synod issued a legal opinion way back in 'January 1991, revised September 2003' on ['Holy Communion: administration of the Sacrament'](https://www.churchofengland.org/about/leadership-and-governance/legal-resources/legal-opinions-and-other-guidance/legal-opinions#calibre_link-654) ([also available as a PDF](https://www.churchofengland.org/sites/default/files/2024-09/reception-of-communion.pdf)). This includes a lot of detail about individual cups and a smaller amount about intinction. However, the current version includes the following note at the top:

> At its meeting on 13 June 2024, the Legal Advisory Commission noted that there are two respectable views on the matter of the use of individual cups at Holy Communion.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, Church of England guidance was issued in mid-2020 on ['Holy Communion and the distribution of the elements'](https://www.churchofengland.org/sites/default/files/2020-07/holy-communion-distribution-of-the-elements.pdf), in which the common cup was suspended. This led to an intense debate on the use of individual cups:

* Various responses are linked from [a Thinking Anglicans blog post](https://www.thinkinganglicans.org.uk/individual-cups-at-holy-communion/) of 27 August 2020 ...
* ... including [a legal opinion on 'The legality of the use of individual cups for communion wine in the Church of England'](https://www.thinkinganglicans.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Holy-Communion-Opinion-on-individual-cups-12.8.2020.pdf) (also [here](https://www.lawandreligionuk.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/200812-The-legality-of-the-use-of-individual-cups-for-communion-wine.pdf)).
* Thomas Renz published [numerous articles on the topic on his own blog](https://hadleyrectory.blogspot.com/search/label/Holy%20Communion), as well as [a post on the Psephizo blog](https://www.psephizo.com/life-ministry/should-we-withhold-the-giving-of-wine-in-holy-communion/)
* [A post on the Law & Religion UK blog from March 2021](https://lawandreligionuk.com/2021/03/18/coronavirus-and-ecclesiastical-law-ii/) contains links to various resources.
* Andrew Atherstone wrote a Grove Booklet on the topic, [_'Drink This, All of You': Individual Cups at Holy Communion_](https://grovebooks.co.uk/product/w-250-drink-this-all-of-you-individual-cups-at-holy-communion-2022/), which is described in [a post on the Psephizo blog](https://www.psephizo.com/life-ministry/history-law-and-individual-cups-at-communion/).

Subsequent Church of England guidance was issued in December 2020 regarding intinction, using the language of 'simultaneous administration', where the priest (rather than the communicant) dips the wafer into the wine (otherwise known as 'priestly intinction'). That guidance is available [on the Thinking Anglicans website](https://www.thinkinganglicans.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Holy-Communion-letter-and-guidance-011220.pdf) (linked from [a blog post](https://www.thinkinganglicans.org.uk/further-advice-from-the-archbishops-on-holy-communion-and-its-distribution/)).

The Church of England website still contains a page with [Coronavirus (COVID-19) guidance](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/coronavirus-covid-19-guidance). This page includes a link to a July 2022 document, ['Opening and managing church buildings'](https://www.churchofengland.org/sites/default/files/2022-07/covid-19-guidance-v3.2.pdf), which says the following under 'Administration of Holy Communion':

> If communicants do not wish to receive in both kinds, they may do so by receiving only the bread (communion in one kind) or the president may dip the bread in the wine before giving it to the communicant (simultaneous administration). Intinction by the communicant is not recommended.

More recent discussion in the Church of England has been on the topic of gluten-free bread and alcohol-free wine, precipitated by a question posed at General Synod.

* A press release was issued by the Church of England on 10 February 2025: [No, we're not banning 'gluten-free' bread or 'non-alcoholic' Communion wine](https://www.churchofengland.org/media/press-releases/no-were-not-banning-gluten-free-bread-or-non-alcoholic-communion-wine)
* Andrew Atherstone wrote a post on the Law & Religion UK blog, which was published on 17 February 2025: ['Wheat bread and fermented wine at Holy Communion? The origins of Canon B17'](https://lawandreligionuk.com/2025/02/17/wheat-bread-and-fermented-wine-at-holy-communion-the-origins-of-canon-b17/)
* That same blog has an update on the topic from 10 July 2025: ['Progress on those unable to consume gluten and/or alcohol at Holy Communion?'](https://lawandreligionuk.com/2025/07/10/progress-on-those-unable-to-consume-gluten-and-or-alcohol-at-holy-communion/)

Looking a bit further back in the history of the Church of England, Andrew Atherstone had an article published on 19 September 2025 on ['Germ Panic and Chalice Hygiene in the Church of England, c.1895–1930'](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-9809.70010).

Outside the Church of England, there was quite a debate on the topic of intinction in the **Presbyterian Church in America (PCA)** around 2012, [with the 40th General Assembly](https://www.pcahistory.org/pca/ga/actions/40thGA_2012_Actions.pdf) in 2012 giving 'initial approval' to an amendment 'to prohibit the practice of intinction in communion', which was then not approved by the presbyteries and was therefore taken no further, as noted at the [41st General Assembly](https://www.pcahistory.org/pca/ga/actions/41stGA_2013_Actions.pdf) in 2013.

* The most detailed treatment I have found on the topic of intinction is a paper by Lane B. Keister published in 2018 in the Mid-America Journal of Theology (MAJT), ['Intinction: An Historical, Exegetical, and Systematic-Theological Examination'](https://www.midamerica.edu/uploads/files//pdf/journal/06keisterjournal2018.pdf).
* [An earlier (December 2012) version of that paper](https://cameronshaffer.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/IntinctionPaper.pdf) (which contains some additional material at the end) is linked from [a blog post from 20 April 2020 by Cameron Schaffer: 'A Personal Journey on Intinction'](https://cameronshaffer.com/2020/04/20/a-personal-journey-on-intinction/).
* There are [a few posts on intinction on the website of byFaith](https://byfaithonline.com/?s=intinction), which is 'the magazine of the Presbyterian Church in America', such as the following from 2012: ['The Intinction Debate'](https://byfaithonline.com/the-intinction-debate-lets-make-general-assembly-the-last-step-not-the-first/), ['Adiaphora and Intinction'](https://byfaithonline.com/adiaphora-and-intinction/), and ['Update on Intinction Vote: Six in Favor of BCO Change, 12 Against'](https://byfaithonline.com/update-on-intinction-vote-six-in-favor-of-bco-changes-11-against/).
* More recently, [R. Scott Clark has a post from 2021: [John] 'Owen Gives Us Theological Reasons To Object To Intinction'](https://heidelblog.net/2021/01/owen-gives-us-theological-reasons-to-object-to-intinction/), which contains a few helpful links at the end.
* Also from the PCA stable is [a blog post from 29 March 2025 by O. Palmer Robertson: 'Should you Dip the Bread in the Wine at the Lord’s Supper?'](https://elder2elder.substack.com/p/3-should-you-dip-the-bread-in-the).

On the same side of the pond, in **The Episcopal Church**, I came across a couple of articles:

* From 1945, there is [an article by Gardiner Mumford Day on 'The Importance of Intinction'](https://anglicanhistory.org/usa/gmday/intinction1945.html).
* From 2025, there is [an article by Matthew S. C. Olver of The Living Church: 'It's Time to Retire Intinction: An Open Letter to the Bishops of The Episcopal Church'](https://livingchurch.org/covenant/its-time-to-retire-intinction-an-open-letter-to-the-bishops-of-the-episcopal-church/)

That post has some helpful links to the **science**, including ['Holy Communion and Infection Transmission: A Literature Review' by 
Dimitrios Anyfantakis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7377019/) (2020).

In the **Roman Catholic** Church, the laity received only the bread (the host) until 1970 (following Vatican II), after which practice has been governed by the [General Instruction of the Roman Missal](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/ccdds/documents/rc_con_ccdds_doc_20030317_ordinamento-messale_en.html), as described [in](https://www.catholic.com/qa/what-are-the-rules-for-dipping-the-consecrated-host-into-the-precious-blood) [various](https://adoremus.org/2016/11/rite-questions-intinction-allowed/) [places](https://www.catechistcafe.com/liturgy/august-08th-2015).

Plenty to read, if anyone is inclined to delve deeper into the topic!
